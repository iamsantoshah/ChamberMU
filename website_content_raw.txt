2. About Us Page
2.1 Overview
Chamber.MU is an Indian law firm with a unique practice in constitutional law, immigration law, international human rights and humanitarian law, international criminal law and alternate dispute resolution mechanisms including arbitration and mediation. We are a full service law firm and practice all civil and criminal litigations across all courts of India including the Supreme Court of India.
________________


2.2 Our Purpose
Our work particularly focuses on understanding the blend of International law jurisprudence within the domestic constitutional law practice of India. We aim to introduce some shades of brown in the international human rights, humanitarian law and criminal law jurisprudence through locating these universal legal principles within the comparative constitutional law practice and policy advocacy within south asia.
________________


2.3 Our Values
We are a team of feminist lawyers with staunch faith in constitutionalism. Our practice strives to achieve social justice and therefore we do not accept briefs that oppose equality and representation of any marginalised or disenfranchised community across the world. We specialise in strategic feminist constitutional lawyering and provide the best available representation to each client irrespective of their social status.
Our particular aim is to provide our marginalised and disenfranchised clients the same standard of legal representation that is afforded to affluent clients.

2. About Us Page
2.1 Overview
The Lingering Shadows Initiative (LSI) is a program to amplify the voices of the disenfranchised and marginalised social groups through litigation within courtrooms and through reporting, advocacy, art, storytelling, theatre and films outside the courtrooms.


Ujjaini Chatterji and Mayurapriyan Thangavel—Founders, The Lingering Shadows Initiative
In 2022, Ujjaini Chatterji, along with Mayurapriyan Thangavel, Advocate, founded the Lingering Shadows Initiative through which their Team of Advocates and paralegals regularly visit the communities of migrant workers and refugees, record their stories and narrative for publication in various mediums. The team of advocates also provides extensive legal consultations, representations and training, to the workers, women and refugees living in Delhi NCR, Assam and Tamil Nadu, which impacts 1500 families across India.
What started off as an academic endeavour has blossomed into a pro-bono litigation program that extends legal and paralegal support to the nameless, stateless persons living on the fringes of bustling cities; battling for mere survival from the margins of civil societies, and lingering in the shadows of tall rights of freedom and liberty asking to be counted, recognised and treated as humans. 
About the Founders
Ujjaini Chatterji is a New Delhi–based human rights lawyer specialising in strategic constitutional litigation and feminist human rights lawyering and refugee rights. She represents Rohingya women and children before Indian courts. Ujjaini argued in landmark cases including the case of Sabera Khatoon v. Union of India, a landmark Supreme Court case affirming refugee rights to healthcare and protection from indefinite detention. She is also leading and arguing the public interest litigation in the Supreme Court of India against custodial deaths and indefinite detention of Rohingya refugees in India. Ujjaini is a “Constitution Defender Fellow” at the Centre for Law and Policy Research and a two-time delegate to the UN Commission on the Status of Women. She holds an LL.M. in Human Rights Law from the University of Nottingham and has trained with the European Inter-University Centre.  In 2026, Ujjaini was awarded the “Your Nottingham Alumni Award” for her contribution as an advocate and international human rights lawyer supporting displaced and refugee communities. [Watch on YouTube here]
Mayurapriyan Thangavel is a New Delhi–based advocate specialising in feminist constitutional law and strategic human rights litigation. His practice focuses on representing Dalit, Bahujan, tribal, Muslim, and Rohingya refugee communities, with an emphasis on issues of statelessness, indefinite detention and deportation. He led the constitutional challenge to the Citizenship Amendment Act, 2019, and its 2024 Rules, on behalf of Member of Parliament P. Sandosh Kumar, arguing its incompatibility with India’s constitutional guarantees of equality and non-discrimination. Mayurapriyan also represents Rohingya women and children before the Delhi High Court and the Supreme Court of India. He was nominated as a delegate to the 2025 United Nations Commission on the Status of Women. A law graduate from Symbiosis International University (2017), he is also a regular columnist writing on equality, Dravidian politics, constitutionalism, and scientific temper. He is also an artist and an actor [check his profile here].  His paintings have been exhibited across embassies and different galleries. As an actor, he performs in various theatre productions and short films in English and Tamil language.
Ujjaini and Mayurapriyan collaborate on constitutional litigation, diplomacy, and policy advocacy rooted in social justice. It is through this collaborative effort that the LSI is born and thriving.

2.2 Mission & Vision
The LSI lives its vision by supporting:
* Pro-bono litigation
* Para-legal advisory forums
* Specialised workshops
* Ground report, academic research papers, articles, and digital blogs
* Educational and extra-curricular competitions for school and college students
* Seminars, Talks, Conferences, Webinars, and Panel Discussions
* Representation and Collaboration with International organisations and forums
* Training and T-T-T Sessions (especially on POSH, POCSO, Cyber Crimes, etc.)
* Certificate courses 
* Podcasts
* Theatre, Film-screenings, Storytelling
* Art exhibitions


Globetrotting and Human Rights
LSI is the story of stories it has nurtured, the voice of the voiceless, and the face of faceless non-entities that are trapped in the binaries of statehood. Carrying forward its vision of creating a global network of passionate young lawyers, practitioners, activists, storytellers, artists, and human rights advocates, the founders of LSI live the philosophy of backpackers and vagabonds. The LSI team travels across the world where they cover ground reports and tell stories about different places, through cuisines, adventure sports, treks, swims, art, music, and heritage walks as exercises to explore cultures. 


Way ahead
The team LSI looks forward to organising workshops and retreats in different parts of the world to offer a select few a slice of this life—living, travelling and working in true bohemian style.


Objectives of LSI
* Protection of Stateless persons who are vulnerable to unlawful detentions and even deportation through strategic litigation;
* Strategic litigation to implement the right to life, personal liberty and right to health of detainees in detention centres;
* To build a self sustaining team of dedicated young lawyers and researchers which train to work on issues of citizenship and statelessness beyond the project timelines;
